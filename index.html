01The bench

Every tool a cloning project needs, in one place.

From the first plasmid file to the verified clone, without switching programs. Each item below runs client-side, from short oligos up to genome-scale sequences.

The full feature list, with screenshots →

ASequences

  • Sequence editorCanvas-rendered, linear and circular, from oligos to megabases.
  • Plasmid mapFeatures, cut sites and ORFs on an interactive ring, exported to SVG and PDF.
  • AnnotationA feature panel with filters and CDS checks, and a library that annotates common parts.
  • Find and ORFsIUPAC and protein search in six frames, and a configurable ORF finder.
  • ProteinsProperties, domains, proteolysis, SDS-PAGE and 3D structure.

BDesign

  • PrimersNearest-neighbour Tm, hairpins and dimers, pair search and a primer library.
  • Codon optimizationHost usage tables, genetic codes and motif, GC and repeat constraints.
  • CRISPR guidesScored protospacers, off-target search, base and prime editing, oligos to order.
  • CloningRestriction, Gibson and HiFi, Golden Gate, Gateway, TOPO, on real maps.

CAnalysis

  • Restriction analysisThe REBASE enzyme set, filtered by cuts, overhang and supplier.
  • Virtual gelDigests on a simulated gel, with ladders and a diagnostic digest designer.
  • AlignmentPairwise and multiple alignment with MAFFT, MUSCLE and Kalign, in an editable viewer.
  • BLASTNCBI searches from inside the editor, kept with the sequence.

DReads

  • SangerAB1 and SCF traces, trimming, assembly into contigs and clone verification by the plate.
  • NGS read setsFASTQ import with a FastQC-style quality report, trimming and filtering.
  • Mapping and assemblyminimap2 in WebAssembly, variants with coding effects, de novo assembly, BAM in and out.
  • FiguresPublication figures and PDF export from every view.

02In practice

Three things it does that you would otherwise need three programs for.

Cloning

Plan the construct on the map, then order the oligos.

Click the cut sites on each part's own map and watch the reaction strip assemble the product. Seamless assembly, Golden Gate with overhang fidelity, Gateway and TOPO follow the same path, and the junctions, oligos and verification digest come out at the end.

About cloning →

Reads

Map a FASTQ to your reference without leaving the tab.

Import a read set, read its quality report, trim it, and map it with minimap2 running in WebAssembly. Coverage, reads coloured by strand or pair, and variants with their effect on every coding feature, named the way HGVS names them.

About mapping →

CRISPR

Pick a guide, check it against the whole genome, read the edit back.

Protospacers for a dozen nucleases or one you describe yourself, scored for activity and for the repair the break is likely to get. Off-targets with mismatches and bulges, donors that will not be re-cut, pegRNAs built in their pieces, and the edited chromatogram taken apart against its control.

03Local first

Your sequences never leave your machine.

There is no server. The page you open is the whole program, and every alignment, digest and mapping is computed in your browser.

  • ComputedIn Web Workers and WebAssembly on your own CPU. Long jobs run in the background and can be cancelled.
  • StoredIn your browser's IndexedDB, auto-saved as you work. Export a session file to move it to another machine.
  • OfflineLoad the page once and it keeps working without a connection. Only NCBI, UniProt and BLAST need the network, and only when you ask.
  • OpenThe source is on GitHub under the AGPL. The README explains every method, its parameters and the papers behind it.
